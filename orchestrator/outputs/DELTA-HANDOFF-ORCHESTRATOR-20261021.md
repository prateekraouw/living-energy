## 5. Scientific findings retained from the biology work

### 5.1 Negative results and boundaries

- Added stochastic noise reduced ATP output in the tested ETC model; it did not improve energy production.
- A staging advantage measured per pulse disappeared when normalized per assigned time.
- There is no shared microscopic rate law between mitochondrial electron transfer and hadronic antiproton creation.
- Electromagnetic fields can guide charged antiprotons after creation but cannot select antiprotons from all same-charge, same-rigidity contaminants by species identity.
- Neutrons and photons ignore charge optics. Surviving primary protons and charged/neutral secondaries require explicit dump and shielding paths.
- Conservative electromagnetic optics cannot cool phase space. Cooling requires entropy export and incurs power, time, vacuum and transfer costs.
- Monte Carlo random seeds and artificial cross-section changes are estimator tools, not physical biological control mechanisms.

### 5.2 Defensible biological analogies

| Biological function | Physical architecture analogue | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Selective membrane | Target/vacuum interface plus charge/rigidity selector | Cannot identify antiprotons among identical charge/rigidity tracks |
| Kinetic proofreading | Sequential charge, rigidity, timing and RF cuts | Every cut loses yield and costs apparatus/power |
| Compartmentalization | Separate target/dump, clean transport, deceleration, cooling and storage | Does not increase elementary production probability |
| Waste export | Dedicated primary, neutral and charged-secondary dumps | Heat and radiation are relocated, not removed |
| Damage control | Sacrificial/replaceable first elements | Adds downtime, mass and remote-handling requirements |
| Parallel pathways | Multiple rigidity channels | Splits accepted phase space; does not increase phase-space density |
| Persistent energy structures | Permanent magnets, persistent superconducting currents, high-Q resonators | Stored energy, cryogenics, radiation tolerance and protection still count |

### 5.3 Proposed downstream architectures, not yet tested

1. Asymmetric target/vacuum extraction followed by immediate dispersive charge/rigidity separation.
2. Time-gated capture with phase-synchronous RF or induction deceleration.
3. Compartmented deceleration with cooling/storage plateaus.

All remain proposals. SIM008 fired none of the predeclared energy-midpoint, length, radius, face/interface, segmentation, spot, timing or multi-band triggers.

## 6. Antiproton source physics learned

- Free proton-proton antiproton threshold kinetic energy is about 5.63 GeV. Nuclear Fermi motion and multistep channels can produce subthreshold events but do not remove the steep near-threshold suppression.
- Lower primary energy reduces rigidity and nominal deceleration scale, but the antiproton-production probability falls much faster than the simple `1/T_p` beam-energy advantage near threshold.
- External Sugaya data show 5-to-12 GeV differential cross-section ratios of roughly `10^-3` to `10^-4` in relevant secondary-momentum bins; multiplying by the lower beam-energy advantage still leaves near-threshold yield per joule far lower.
- The current evidence supports an architectural source program only after the production kernel is validated. It does not support a new biological microscopic production mechanism.
